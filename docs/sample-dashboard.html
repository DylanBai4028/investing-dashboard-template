Cross-border tax research

Mechanisms explained and scenarios modeled using this template's sample numbers — not a substitute for advice from a registered tax agent, and nothing here should be relied on for a real filing or decision. Full sourced write-up with citations: tax/research.md in this repo. Adapt the numbers and facts to your own situation, and get it checked by a professional before relying on any of it.

Are you currently exempt from AU CGT on your shares and crypto?

Possibly — on the facts researched for this worked example (a non-protected Special Category Visa, no disqualifying spouse, no AU PR/citizenship planned), a portfolio like this one could qualify for "temporary resident" CGT treatment. That means CGT event I1 — the rule that deems your whole portfolio sold the moment you cease AU tax residency — could not apply at all. Meridian, Northbridge, and Crypto holdings are not Australian property, so as a temporary resident those gains were never in the Australian CGT net to begin with.

This flips the moment any of three things change: an AU citizen/PR spouse, an AU PR grant, or citizenship. It's also the single finding in this research most worth a professional second opinion for your own facts — the primary ATO source couldn't be reached directly during this research (see the doc for why); this rests on secondary sources that agree with each other, not primary confirmation.

AU/NZ residency tests + DTA tie-breaker

Two independent questions, not one: what ends AU tax residency (a holistic "did your whole life move" test, no bright line), and separately what starts NZ residency (a much more mechanical 183-day test — easier to trigger than to escape again, since ceasing NZ residency needs 325+ days absent and no home there). The tax treaty's tie-breaker resolves which country gets taxing rights if both apply at once — it does not by itself end Australian residency under Australia's own law, which is what actually triggers CGT event I1.

AU CGT event I1 — deemed disposal

Deems every non-property asset sold at market value the day AU residency ends — unless you're a temporary resident (see above), in which case it doesn't apply. SouthernCross gets its own separate exclusion regardless. Kowhai likely doesn't get that same carve-out (it's a NZ scheme, not Australian super) — it'd fall back to ordinary treatment. A deferral election is available too: keep treating an asset as taxable Australian property until it's actually sold, pushing the tax point out indefinitely.

NZ FIF regime

If NZ tax resident again, foreign shares/ETFs over a NZD $50,000 cost threshold get taxed on a deemed annual return (5% of opening value, or actual gain — your choice each year), not on realised gains. Two exemptions that might sound relevant don't actually apply here: the Australian-shares exemption doesn't reach Meridian's holdings (they're US-listed, not ASX-listed companies), and the transitional-resident exemption needs 10+ years overseas — well short of a near-term return.

US estate tax exposure

Non-resident aliens get only a US$60,000 exemption on US-situs assets (direct US shares/ETFs — Northbridge and Meridian's US positions both qualify; crypto's situs is a genuine unsettled question). A US-Australia treaty extends a much larger exemption, but only if "domiciled" in Australia — a different, higher bar than tax residency, and genuinely fact-specific. No equivalent NZ-US treaty exists. The highest-dollar open question in the whole research doc.
